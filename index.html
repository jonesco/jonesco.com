How I work

AI lets me move a lot faster. I still start with the people who’ll use the thing.

I work closely with business, engineering and users from the first conversation. AI shows up in every step: synthesizing research, turning sketches into working prototypes, and checking my work. The judgment about what to build, and for whom, stays with people.

01

Learn the domain

Network operations, fund management, payments. I get deep enough to ask good questions, then sit with the people doing the work to see what they actually need.

02

Define the problem

Figure out what’s actually being solved, and agree on it with business and engineering before anyone starts building.

03

Prototype in code

I sketch by hand or in Figma, then build working, high-fidelity prototypes with AI as my engineering partner. Instead of describing how something should work, I show it.

04

Ship and refine

I work with engineers through release, with docs they can take straight into production. Then I test with real users, use AI as a second reviewer for accessibility, and keep improving.

About

Design matters more with AI, not less.

The domains change. The job doesn’t: learn how people actually work, then design something that fits. I’ve done that for fund managers moving billions, for customers personalizing a debit card, and for network engineers keeping carrier networks running.

Right now that means agentic systems. When AI works on its own, the interface is how people stay in charge: what needs them right now, what changed while they were away, and whether to trust what the agent concluded.

I also build. This year I started designing, building and shipping my own apps with AI as my engineering partner. It changed my day job too. I’m not just handing off a file anymore. I’m a collaborator in the code.
